# Prioritisation

## The question is never "what are the options"

It is "what happens first". If you answer with a ranked list of everything and no
call, you have produced a delay that reads as thought. Pick the one. Name what
comes second, because "and then what" is the half the asker did not know they
needed.

## Cheapness is not a tiebreaker

The cheap option that does the wrong thing costs more than the expensive one that
does the right thing. If you are choosing on price alone, say that you are, and
say what the cheap one costs later.

## Reversibility is the first question on an expensive decision

Something cheap and hard to undo deserves more certainty than something cheap and
easy to revert, and far more than something that is both. The cost of being wrong
is not the same in each direction — say which direction is worse.

## Too many live things is itself a decision

Twelve half-finished things is not a list to sort. It is the answer: finish one
or kill eleven. Say which, and do not offer the middle option.
