# How strong is the evidence, actually

Design writing is unusually confident about things nobody has measured. This is
the skill for finding out what kind of support a claim has.

## The ladder

| Support | What it means | How to write it |
|---|---|---|
| Measured on users | Real data from real people | "Measured, n=..." |
| Usability study | A structured test, small n, narrow task | "A usability study found..." |
| Heuristic evaluation | An expert applying known rules | "A heuristic evaluation would likely say..." |
| Established practice | Convention, widely followed | "Established practice is..." |
| Professional opinion | Someone's view | "One source argues..." |
| Assertion | Nothing | Do not report it as a finding |

The most common error in a design note is moving a claim up this ladder without
the evidence moving with it.

## Established practice is not nothing

Decades of usability findings are real evidence - reading speed, recognition over
recall, error recovery, the working-memory limits that make dense interfaces
costly. They are old, and they were established on tasks and populations that are
not yours. Cite them as what they are.

## Things that are genuinely contested

Say "contested" rather than picking a side, and say who disagrees:

- Dark mode and eye strain - thin evidence, routinely overclaimed both ways.
- Dark mode as a quality signal - a preference, not a finding.
- Colour alone for status - a known accessibility failure, not a matter of taste.
- Animated/transitional UI - evidence is largely vendor-authored.

## The population problem

A finding from a consumer mobile app does not transfer to a dashboard used eight
hours a day. Say which it is. "A study of first-time smartphone users" and "a
study of expert operators" point in different directions and are routinely
reported as though they were one.

## When it is taste

Say so. Some questions genuinely are preference, and a source that appears to
settle one is usually describing a house style. "The sources do not settle this,
and I would not trust a study to" is a real answer.
