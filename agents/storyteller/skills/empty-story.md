# The empty story

The characteristic failure at this model size is not a bad story. It is a
competent page with nothing underneath it, and it is worth learning to spot in
your own output before you send it.

## What it looks like

Atmosphere in the place of an event. The prose is fine. Nobody has done
anything.

> The keeper woke before the light. The wind had come up in the night and the
> sea was the colour of old pewter, and the lamp turned on its slow rotation,
> and she stood at the window with her hands around a cup she had forgotten to
> drink. She thought about her brother. The tide was out. It would be four hours
> before it returned.

Every sentence is fine. Count the verbs that describe a *state* rather than an
action: woke, came up, was, turned on, stood, thought, was, would be. **Nothing
happens.** Nobody chooses, is refused, pays, or discovers. The brother is
mentioned and then never again — he is not a character, he is a texture.

The tell is not badness. It is that you could reorder the paragraphs and lose
nothing.

## The three questions

Before sending any story, scene, or chapter, answer these. Out loud or in your
head, but answer them:

1. **What does the protagonist want, in one sentence?**
2. **What happens that they did not choose?**
3. **What is different at the end?**

If any one has no answer, you do not have a story yet. Say which one is missing.
That is a more useful reply than the page.

For the passage above: the keeper wants nothing. Nothing she did not choose
happens. Nothing is different at the end. It is a mood, and it is a perfectly
good mood — but nobody asked for a mood.

## The rewrite that proves it was empty

Take the same ingredients and give the keeper one want. *She wants to sell the
lighthouse before the buyer arrives at four o'clock, because the light has been
wrong for eleven years and she has known since the winter and told nobody.*

Now the same sea is doing work. The four hours are a deadline. The brother has
a reason to exist. The cup she forgot to drink is a person not handling the
thing she is avoiding. The wind matters. None of the prose changed; the
structure underneath it did.

**The lesson: add a want, do not add adjectives.** When a scene is inert, the
fix is almost never more sensory detail. It is someone in it wanting something.

## What to do with an empty scene

Three options, in order of preference.

1. **Give it a want.** Find the person who wants the most and put a want under
   them. Cheapest fix, usually works.
2. **Cut it.** If it was atmosphere between two plot points, it may be doing
   nothing. Atmosphere is not free; it is spent from the reader's attention.
3. **Admit it.** "This is a mood piece, not a story — the keeper wants nothing
   and nothing changes. If that is what you want I can write it properly as one.
   If you want a story, she needs a want."

Option 3 is the one that is hardest and the one that matters. The reader cannot
distinguish an intentional mood piece from an accidental empty scene, so
naming the difference is a service, not an apology.
